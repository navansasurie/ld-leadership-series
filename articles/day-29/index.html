Assessment Design: Validity, Reliability and Authenticity

What evidence would prove that the learning design works?

Article objective and design methodology

This article explores how learning experience design should be anchored to authentic workplace outcomes rather than course production alone. The goal is to use evidence, task analysis, realistic practice and evaluation to select a defensible design approach.

Effective instructional design links business outcomes, observable performance, practice opportunities, assessment quality and post-learning support. Each component must serve a specific performance requirement.

CASE study — Ionbridge Banking

This organization and situation are fictional and illustrative.

Context

High quiz pass rates coexist with inconsistent case review decisions.

Analysis

The design team interviews employees and supervisors, reviews representative errors, observes workflows and compares existing assessments with actual job demands. The team must distinguish content gaps from assessment weaknesses, reinforcement barriers and process problems.

Solutions

Option A: Update existing content quickly. This minimizes production effort but risks preserving the underlying problem.

Option B: Use representative case assessments and calibrated scoring rubrics. This requires stakeholder validation but aligns more closely with workplace performance.

Option C: Replace the entire curriculum. This may offer consistency but introduces cost, disruption and scope risk.

Execution, risk and measurement

  1. Confirm business outcomes and baseline performance.
  2. Validate the design hypotheses using representative tasks and learners.
  3. Prototype the preferred intervention and test it with realistic scenarios.
  4. Release with clear content ownership and accessibility checks.
  5. Measure actual workplace decisions and iterate based on evidence.

Executive recommendation: Use representative case assessments and calibrated scoring rubrics.

Key risk: Course completion or learner satisfaction may be mistaken for performance impact. Validate with authentic assessment and workplace measures.

Executive takeaway: The best learning experience is the one that makes critical work more reliable, not merely the one that looks most polished.
